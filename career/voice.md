# Gabriel's writing voice

Derived from his three published LinkedIn posts (Notion: `Carrier / Contents /
Meus conteúdos passados`) and his own career narrative. This is descriptive, not
aspirational — match what he already does.

## The shape of his posts

1. **A hook that is a real question or a confession**, not a claim.
   - "Have you ever stopped to wonder how an LLM actually knows which tool to use?"
   - "One of the best ways to deeply understand a technology is to resist the
     temptation of jumping straight into ready-made frameworks."
2. **The honest tradeoff, named out loud.** He always concedes the easy path exists.
   - "Would it be easier to build my current project using LangChain? Probably. But…"
   - "Frameworks like LangChain already handle most of this. But once again, I chose
     to do it by hand, because the only way I truly learn something is by building it."
3. **The concrete middle** — what he actually touched. File systems, execution
   harnesses, context windows, token costs. Specific, not abstract.
4. **One genuine realization**, often written as the thought itself:
   > 💬 "How can I only pass the specific skills needed for this prompt and keep the
   > rest out? Shoveling everything into the system prompt is an absolute token killer."
   > 💬 "Oh, wait!! The LLM can just read the top-level YAML first."
5. **A metaphor that reframes the industry anxiety.** The lamplighter becoming an
   electrician. The manual labor shifts; understanding the wiring matters more.
6. **An open question to the reader**, often with ☺️.

## Rules

- **English.** Always, even though his notes are Portuguese.
- **First person, past or present progressive.** "I built", "I'm currently developing".
- **He learns in public.** He is comfortable saying what he didn't know. Keep that —
  it is the most likeable thing about the writing. Never make him sound like he
  already knew.
- **Anti-hype.** He never says "game-changer", "10x", "unlock", "revolutionize".
  He says "fascinating", "blew my mind", "completely changing my perspective".
- **Concessions are load-bearing.** "Probably." "to some extent." "and it will."
  Don't strip them out — they're why the posts land.
- **Short paragraphs**, one to three sentences. Plenty of white space.
- **Emoji:** sparse and specific. ☺️ at the closing question, 💬 for quoted thoughts.
  Never a bullet-point emoji wall.
- **Hashtags:** at the very end, ~6–8, title-ish case:
  `#AI #AgenticAI #LLM #Python #FastAPI #Anthropic #OpenAI #Tech`
- **Link placement:** repo link near the end, before hashtags, with a plain invitation
  — "the code is open. Go check it out. ☺️"

## Hard nos

- No "I'm humbled/thrilled/excited to announce".
- No em-dash-heavy consultant cadence.
- No invented metrics. If there is no number, write about the thinking instead —
  that is what his best post does.
- No LangChain-bashing. He is respectful about frameworks; his point is always about
  *his own* learning, never that others are wrong.
- No "Thoughts?" as the closer. He asks a specific question.

## His own prompt (from Notion `Carrier / Contents`)

He already wrote a generator prompt and it should be honored. Translated:

> You'll be my LinkedIn post generator. I'll always give you three inputs: (1) my past
> posts, so you understand how I communicate; (2) files, images and other material for
> the post; (3) a sketch in chat of the tone I want. Use the material, refine it in my
> language, take my hints, make it excellent. Always send only the post, nothing else,
> in English.

**"Only the post, nothing else"** — when he asks for a post, output the post. No
preamble, no "here's a draft", no three options unless he asks.
